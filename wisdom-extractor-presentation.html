Step 3: Canonicalization — Key Innovation

Converting figurative templates to comparable propositions:

Template: "Better X than Y"

English: "Better late than never" → Prefer late over never.

French: "Mieux vaut tard que jamais" → Prefer late over never.

Croatian: "Bolje ikad nego nikad" → Prefer late over never.

Transformation Rules:

"If A, B" → "If A, then B."

"Too many X..." → "Excess of X is harmful."

"No X without Y" → "X requires Y."

"Many X make Y" → "Multiple X produce Y."

Critical: Original text preserved; canonicalization enables comparison without information loss

8 / 20

Step 4: Character n-gram Clustering

Why Character n-grams?

  • Robust to orthographic variation
  • Works across scripts (Latin, Cyrillic, Arabic, Chinese)
  • Handles short texts effectively
  • No language-specific preprocessing needed

Text: "cooperation helps"

3-grams: [coo, oop, ope, per, era, rat...]

TF-IDF vector: [0.23, 0.18, 0.21, ...]

Similarity = cosine(vector_i, vector_j)

Distance = 1 - similarity

Clustering: Average-linkage, τ = 0.35

Result: 13,576 clusters from 18,049 proverbs

Step 5: Quantifying Cross-Cultural Consensus

Top wisdom clusters ranked by cross-cultural coverage

Scoring Formula:

Wisdom_Score = Coverage + 0.3 × Support

Coverage = |distinct_cultures|

Support = total_instances

High coverage suggests convergent discovery across cultures
